# Controls

[Back to the front page](../README.md)

## Play with a gamepad, a keyboard or a mouse

A gamepad, the keyboard and the mouse all work, even at the same time.
They all control Ico. A second gamepad controls Yorda when the game's
two-player mode is on (Options > Gameplay > Players, on every disc once you
have finished the game); the first gamepad to be plugged in is Ico's, and if
you unplug it, the next one you plug in takes its place.

Gamepad buttons go by where they sit, not by what is printed on them. The
bottom face button is always Cross, whether your pad is an Xbox,
PlayStation or Switch pad. In the menus the gamepad's four face buttons
and the d-pad always go by position, even after you change them under
Remap controls: the bottom button confirms, the top button goes back, and
the d-pad moves (on the Japanese, Korean and Asian discs the right button confirms and the
bottom one goes back, see [The Japanese, Korean and Asian discs](#the-japanese-korean-and-asian-discs)). Start,
Select, the shoulder buttons, the triggers and the
stick presses follow your changes everywhere, unless you move one of them
onto a face button or the d-pad. Then the game follows your change in
play, but the menus still use that face button or d-pad direction for its
usual job, and the original button for its own.

| PS2 button | Gamepad | Keyboard | Mouse |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cross (confirm, jump, call Yorda) | bottom face button (A on Xbox) | Space | left button |
| Circle (in menus, goes back like Triangle) | right face button | E | right button |
| Square | left face button | Q | |
| Triangle (back) | top face button | R | |
| L1 / R1 (R1 holds Yorda's hand) | shoulder buttons | Tab or the ` key (left of 1) / F | middle button (R1) |
| L2 / R2 | triggers | Z / X | |
| L3 / R3 (press a stick in) | press a stick in | V / B | |
| Start / Select | start / back (or view) | Enter / Backspace | |
| D-pad (menus) | d-pad | arrow keys | |
| Left stick (move) | left stick | W A S D (hold Left Shift to walk) | |
| Right stick (look) | right stick | I J K L | move the mouse |

On the European, Japanese, Korean and Asian discs you keep R1 held to hold
Yorda's hand. On the North American disc, the August 2001 preview and the
June 2001 demo you press it once to take her hand and again to let go. To
play the other way, choose **A (hold)** or **B (toggle)** under
**Options > Controls > Hold type**, from the title screen or the pause
menu; **Disc's own** goes back to the disc's way. It works the same with
every way of playing.

A few more keys on a computer:

- **Escape** opens the pause menu while you play, and goes back in the
  menus, like Triangle. It does not close the game: to quit, choose
  **End Game** in the pause menu, then **Quit to desktop** on the title
  screen, or close the window.
- **Start** skips a movie, on every input: the gamepad's Start button,
  the key you gave to Start (Enter at first) or the Start button on the
  touch screen. No other button skips one.
- **Alt+Enter** switches between a window and the whole screen (the
  Borderless or Fullscreen choice you made under Window mode).
- **F12** saves a picture of the current moment, and a zip file to attach
  to a bug report. See
  [When something goes wrong](TROUBLESHOOTING.md#save-a-picture-of-a-problem).
- **Shift+F12**, in photo mode, saves the paused moment at five picture
  shapes, from 4:3 to the widest, into one zip file beside the F12 files,
  for problem reports about wide screens.
- **F11** makes the game write its speed numbers into the log every second
  for 30 seconds, instead of every 10 seconds. Press it again to stop
  early. It is for problem reports about slow play.

Escape, Alt+Enter, F11, F12 and Shift+F12 cannot be changed. Everything else can be, in
**Options > Controls > Remap controls**. When you give a button or key to
another action, the two actions swap. If the action you changed had no
button on that device, the other action is left without one.
The game's own **Button configuration** applies on top of your changes.

## The Japanese, Korean and Asian discs

On the Japanese disc (SCPS-11003), the Korean disc (SCPS-56001) and the Asian
disc (SCPS-55001) the menus swap two buttons, as those PS2 releases did: **Circle confirms and Cross goes back**.
This covers the game's own menus and the
ones this port adds (Options, the title screen's extras, the model viewer,
Character Customization), and the button pictures in the menus' hints
follow it. The buttons and keys themselves stay where they are: the bottom
face button is still Cross (Space on the keyboard) and the right face
button is still Circle (E on the keyboard), so on a gamepad the right face
button confirms and the bottom face button goes back, and on the keyboard E
confirms and Space goes back (unless you changed them under Remap
controls). The mouse works the same with every disc: the left button
chooses, the right button goes back.
In the menus this port adds (Options, the title screen's extras, the
model viewer, Character Customization), Triangle still goes back too; the
game's own menus go back with Cross only, as on those PS2 releases. Escape
(and Back on Android) goes back as Cross does.

In photo mode, Circle saves a picture, and Cross, Triangle or Start
leaves. The mouse still saves with the left button and leaves with the
right one.

The **Circle goes back** row is not in Options with the Japanese, Korean or
Asian disc, since Circle confirms there.

Where this page says to press Cross in a menu, press Circle on the
Japanese, Korean or Asian disc, and where it says Circle goes back, press Cross.

The European disc, the North American disc, the August 2001 preview disc and
the June 2001 magazine demo confirm with Cross and go back with Triangle, as
this page describes everywhere else.

## Look around with the mouse

On a computer, the mouse moves the camera while you play. The pointer is
hidden then, and moving the mouse turns the camera the way the right stick
does. Keep the mouse still for a moment and the camera swings back behind
Ico on its own, just like letting go of the stick.

The pointer comes back whenever a menu is open and when you switch to
another window. It stays out of the way during films.

You can change how it feels in **Options > Controls**:

- **Mouse camera** Off stops the mouse from turning the camera and lets
  the pointer leave the window while you play. The mouse buttons still
  work.
- **Mouse sensitivity** sets how far the camera turns for the same move of
  the mouse.
- **Invert mouse up/down** swaps up and down.
- **Mouse camera speed** is how quickly the camera reaches the place you
  point it at. 1.0x is the speed the camera has with a gamepad; a higher
  number is quicker, a lower one is slower, and Instant moves it almost
  at once.
- **Mouse camera range** Normal keeps each area's own limit on how far the
  camera can turn. Full lets you look all the way around. In scenes where
  the game steers the camera itself, Full can show what the camera was
  kept from showing.
- **Camera swings back** Off keeps the camera where you left it until you
  move the mouse again, instead of swinging back behind Ico.

These rows are not shown on phones and tablets. The speed and the range
only apply while the mouse is turning the camera; a gamepad or the keys
move it at the normal speed, within each area's own limit.

## Use the mouse in menus

The mouse works in every menu: the title screen, the pause menu, the
memory card and save screens, and all of Options.

- **Point** at a choice to pick it, just like moving to it with the d-pad.
- **Click** it with the left button to choose it.
- To change a setting, click the small arrows on either side of it, or
  click the setting itself to go to the next choice.
- **Scroll the wheel** to move up and down, one line per notch. Long lists
  scroll along.
- The **right button** goes back, like Circle.

Clicking an empty spot does nothing, so a stray click never chooses
anything. If you press a key or a gamepad button, the pointer hides until
you move the mouse again. While the title screen is still checking the
memory card, the choices wait until it is done, for the mouse just as
for a gamepad.

## Look at the models

The model viewer lets you look closely at the characters and some of the
objects. Open it from the title screen: **Options > Extras > Models**.
Pick a model from the list and press Cross.

| To do this | Press |
| --- | --- |
| Turn the model | right stick |
| Move the model around the screen | left stick |
| Zoom out / zoom in | L2 / R2 (keyboard Z / X) |
| Pick the previous or next animation | L1 / R1, or Up / Down |
| Play the animation | Cross |
| Repeat the animation over and over, or stop repeating | Square |
| Go back to the list | Triangle or Circle |

With Developer mode on, **Select** (Backspace on a keyboard) also saves the
model's files for people who make model packs. A message tells you how many
files it saved and where. [More about model
packs](MODEL_PACKS.md#make-a-model-pack).

## Change the colours of Ico and Yorda

**Options > Extras > Character Customization** has a row for each part you
can recolour. On the title screen the character is shown beside its own
rows, and each colour you pick shows on the model at once.

| To do this | Press |
| --- | --- |
| Pick a row | Up / Down |
| Pick a colour for the row | Left / Right |
| Put the row back to its original colour | Square |
| Show the other character (title screen) | L1 / R1, or Cross on **Switch to Yorda** / **Switch to Ico** |
| Turn the model (title screen) | right stick |
| Zoom out / zoom in (title screen) | L2 / R2 (keyboard Z / X) |
| Go back | Triangle or Circle |

From the title screen, going back returns you to the title screen with
Options open on Extras. In the pause menu, the game behind the menu
changes colour as you choose. [All the rows](OPTIONS.md#character-customization).

## Take a photo

Photo mode pauses the game and shows it from a camera you move around,
with every effect the game has, so you can save a picture. During play,
press Start and choose **Photo mode**.

The camera starts as a free camera. You fly it around like a drone:

| To do this | Press |
| --- | --- |
| Move forward, back and to the sides | left stick |
| Look around | right stick, or move the mouse |
| Rise / sink | Up / Down |
| Tilt the picture | L1 / R1 |
| Zoom out / zoom in | L2 / R2 |
| Change the speed (Slow, Normal, Fast) | R3, press the right stick in (keyboard B) |
| Put the camera back where it started | Select |
| Save a picture | Cross, or click the left mouse button |
| Hide or show the help panel | Square |
| Film effect: Off, 1, 2, 3, 4, Deja vu, Off | Right on the D-pad (keyboard Right arrow) |
| Leave photo mode | Triangle, Circle or Start, or click the right mouse button |

Press **L3** (press the left stick in, or keyboard V) to switch to the
orbit camera. It circles around the spot you were looking at:

- The left stick, or moving the mouse, circles around that spot.
- The right stick moves nearer, farther and to the sides.
- Up and Down zoom.

Press L3 again to go back to the free camera. Select puts back only the
camera you are using.

The help panel at the bottom tells you which camera you are using and its
speed, and shows each button beside what it does: the game's own button
pictures, and the names of the buttons it has no picture for (the sticks,
L3, R3, Up, Down, Right and Select). If you play on the keyboard, it shows the
names of your keys instead, like [Space] (the panel changes as soon as
you press a key or a mouse button). When you hide
it with Square, the game remembers, and photo mode opens with the panel
hidden next time too. Press Square again to show it.

The Film effect is a filter here: each press of Right goes to the next
look, from Off through 1 to 4, then Deja vu, and back to Off, and a small
note says which one you have. Deja vu is the grainy veil and the film
scratches of Ico's vision: the two shimmering sheets of grain the game lays
over the picture during that scene, from edge to edge on a wide screen,
and the flickering specks and thin vertical lines of an old film reel that
come and go over the middle of the picture, each at its own small size and
in its own place as in the vision, never repeated across a wide screen,
here over any place you are. During the vision itself the game already
shows them, so Deja vu adds nothing there. The Film effect works whether or not you have
finished the game, and it does not change the Film effect row in Options.
The game remembers your choice for the next time you open photo mode, and
your saved pictures include the effect.

If you move the camera quickly into another room, you may see parts the
game had not prepared yet.

Your pictures are saved as PNG files in a `screenshots` folder, in the
same folder as your saves. [Where that is](PORTABLE_MODE.md#where-your-saves-are).

You can change how fast the camera moves, or swap up and down when you look
around. Close the game, open `config.toml` (it is next to your saves, [where
that is](PORTABLE_MODE.md#where-your-saves-are)) in a text editor, and find
the `[photo]` part. For example, this line makes the camera move half as
fast:

```
stick_speed = 0.5
```

and this one swaps up and down:

```
invert_y = true
```

## Touch controls (phones and tablets)

On a phone or tablet, buttons are drawn on the screen over the game, laid
out like a PlayStation controller. They carry the game's own button
pictures:

- **The stick** is in the lower left. Put your thumb down anywhere in the
  lower left part of the screen, and that spot becomes the middle of the
  stick. Slide your thumb to walk. Slide it out to the ring around the
  stick to run.
  As with a real stick, running between the eight main directions
  needs **Options > Controls > Analogue stick fix** (it is also under
  Gameplay).
- **The D-pad** is above the stick, beside the L1 and L2 buttons in the
  top left corner, with L2 at the top. It moves around the menus.
- **Cross, Circle, Square and Triangle** are in the lower right corner.
- **R1** is the big button just above them. It holds Yorda's hand: keep a
  finger on it, or, with **Hold type** on **B (toggle)** (the North
  American disc's and the 2001 previews' own way), tap it once to take her
  hand and tap it again to let go.
- **R2** is in the top right corner.
- **Start and Select** are at the top in the middle.
- **The look pad** is the rest of the upper right part of the screen.
  Slide a finger there to look around, as you would with the right stick.

The buttons have about the same real size on every phone and tablet. On a
small screen they shrink a little so nothing overlaps. On a foldable,
nothing sits on the fold. When the fold runs from top to bottom, each
hand's buttons stay on their own side of it, flat or half-folded. When it
runs across the screen: half-folded like a laptop, all the buttons move to
the lower half; opened flat, they stay where they are, unless one would sit
on the fold: then they all move below it.

You can use several fingers at once, for example the stick and a button.

When no connected gamepad can rumble, the phone itself vibrates where a
gamepad would rumble. **Options > Controls > Vibration** (in the pause menu's
Options) turns that off.

The buttons fade away 5 seconds after you last touched the screen. Touch
the screen and they come back. When you connect a gamepad, they go away
and the screen stops acting as buttons, so a stray finger does nothing.

**Options > Controls > Touch screen** holds everything for them. The link
only shows up on a device with a touch screen. The page has these rows:

- **Touch controls:** Auto is the normal choice and works as described
  above. Always keeps the buttons on the screen all the time, and they
  keep working with a gamepad connected. Off turns them off, but a phone
  does not offer Off: with no gamepad connected there would be no way to
  turn the buttons back on, so on a phone Off counts as Auto.
- **Touch size:** Small, Medium or Large. Medium is about the size of a
  gamepad's buttons; on small phones Large cannot grow much.
- **Touch opacity:** how see-through they are, from 25 % (faint) to 100 %
  (solid).
- **Edit layout:** opens the editor below. It is dimmed while Touch
  controls is Off.
- **Snap to grid** and **Grid size** (2, 4 or 8 mm): whether a button you
  let go of snaps to the grid, and how wide the grid is.
- **Reset layout:** puts the buttons of the way the phone is held or
  folded right now back to where the game puts them.
- **Reset all layouts:** does the same for every way the phone can be held
  or folded.

### Arranging the buttons yourself

If the buttons are in your way, **Edit layout** lets you move them, make
them bigger or smaller and more or less see-through. While the editor is
open the buttons are drawn at full strength so you can see what you are
doing. A button you made hidden or switched off shows as a dashed outline
with the word Hidden or Off, so you can still tap it to pick it again.

The editor's keys are a thin strip along the top of the screen, and its
tips sit in a line at the bottom, so most of the picture stays clear while
you arrange the buttons.

- **Select:** touch a button. It gets a bright outline and its name. Tap an
  empty spot to select nothing.
- **Move:** drag it. A short touch counts as a tap, not a move. The button
  cannot leave the screen, and it cannot sit on a fold. When you let go it
  snaps to the grid, if Snap is on; the dots show the grid.
- **Resize:** put two fingers on a button and pinch to resize it (the
  second finger can land anywhere once the first is on the button). The
  strip shows its size, such as **Size 120 %**. A button can be 70 % to
  300 % of its normal size; on a small screen a very large button is drawn
  as large as the screen allows. With a gamepad, L2 and R2 make it smaller
  and bigger.
- **See-through:** the **Opacity -** and **Opacity +** keys; the number
  between them is the button's opacity.
- **All:** makes the Opacity keys (and a gamepad's L2 and R2) change every
  button at once.
- **Undo** takes back the last change, up to eight in a row. **Reset** asks
  whether to put back this screen's buttons, every screen's, or nothing.
- **Snap** and **Grid** are the same two settings as on the page.
- **Done** keeps what you see. The Back gesture or button, and Triangle or
  Circle on a gamepad, do the same. Done always keeps what you see; to go
  back, use Undo or Reset first.

So that you never lock yourself out of the menus or the game:

- Any button can be hidden or switched off, the D-pad, Start and the stick
  too. Keep pressing **Opacity -**: after 10 % the button shows **Hidden**
  (you do not see it, but it still works where it was), and one more press
  shows **Off** (it is gone and does nothing). **Opacity +** brings it back
  the same way, Hidden and then 10 %, where you left it.
- If you switch off the D-pad, Cross or Circle, you can tap the menus
  directly instead: the Back gesture or button opens the pause menu during
  play, and a tap on a line picks it. So **Options > Controls > Touch
  screen > Reset layout** stays reachable.
- A button cannot be dropped onto the middle of another one; it jumps
  back and a note says why. Buttons that overlap at the edges are drawn
  in amber, and the one drawn first gets the touch.
- Nothing you do can put a button off the screen, even on another phone or
  after turning the phone: the buttons are always kept on the screen.

A foldable has a separate arrangement for each way it is folded: opened flat, with the fold running top to bottom, and half
folded like a laptop. The editor changes the one the phone is in now, and
the others stay as the game puts them until you edit them as well. If you
fold the phone while editing, the editor moves on to the new way of
holding it and keeps what you did.

Where you put a button is remembered as a real distance from the corners
of the screen, so a layout feels the same on another phone, and the Touch
size setting still makes all of it bigger or smaller.

On a phone with a camera hole, the game's own arrangement keeps its
buttons clear of it, but Edit layout lets you drag a button under the
camera hole, and the dots you snap to reach the glass edge too. A button
goes under the hole once its middle is past the edge of the part of
the screen the game keeps its own buttons in; let go of it with its middle
still inside that part and it stays wholly inside it, against the edge. On
a gamepad, pressing towards the edge once more takes a button there past
it. The buttons you have not moved, and those you placed before this
version, stay where the game puts them. The keys along the top and the
tips at the bottom stay clear of the hole.

A gamepad works in the editor as well, with the touch screen or without
it: L1 and R1 pick the next or the last button, the D-pad or the left
stick moves it one grid step (hold it to repeat), L2 and R2 change the size,
Square and Cross change the opacity (Cross with nothing selected turns All
on and off), Select is Undo, Start asks about Reset (left and right, then
Cross), and Triangle or Circle is Done.

The arrangement is kept in the settings file, in the part called `[touch]`.
If you ever want the game's own arrangement back, use the Reset rows, or
delete the `[touch]` lines from the file with the game closed. In that file
a button is written as `hidden` or `off`. If you write `hidden` into the
settings file by hand, also add the line `format = 2` under `[touch]`. In a
file without that line `hidden` means switched off, so the hidden buttons
of a layout the game saved without it stay switched off, as they were.

On Android, the phone's **Back** button or gesture opens the pause menu,
like Start. [More about Android](ANDROID.md#controls).
